Introduction 
Cores are the fundamental material for understanding underground geological information. The surface 
and internal information of cores such as images, minerals, elements, structures, textures, and physical 
properties can be obtained non-destructively (or micro-destructively), continuously, fast, and in high-
resolution by core digitalization. By core digitalization, the digital data preservation and efficient 
utilization of geological cores can be realized, which can provide technical services and data supports 
for geological survey, mineral resources exploration, ecological environment assessment, geological 
science research, and disaster prevention and mitigation, etc. DD 2022-1 is a series of basic and general 
standards, which can be used as the guidance on core digitalization for the core storage organizations 
and project undertakers. Considering the different working principles, methods, and requirements of 
various types of core digitization technologies, DD 2022-1 is planned consist of the following seven parts 
under the general title Code of practice for core digitalization: 
——Part 1: General, which establishes the general principles and basic requirements for core 
digitalization; 
——Part 2: Surface image digitalization, which establishes operational, traceable and verifiable 
procedures and requirements for core surface image acquisition; 
——Part 3: Spectral scanning, which establishes operational, traceable and verifiable procedures and 
requirements for collecting core spectral reflectance and mineral information; 
——Part 4: X-ray fluorescence element, which establishes operational, traceable and verifiable 
procedures and requirements for collecting core elemental information; 
——Part 5: Multi-scale CT scanning, which establishes operational, traceable and verifiable procedures 
and requirements for collecting core surface and internal structural information; 
——Part 6: Micro-region scanning electron microscope imaging, which establishes operational, traceable 
and verifiable procedures and requirements for collecting core micro-regional surface and internal 
information about mineral, element and structure; 
——Part 7: Physical parameters, which establishes operational, traceable and verifiable procedures and 
requirements for collecting core physical information about resistivity, magnetic susceptibility, etc.

Code of practice for core digitalization - Part 1: General 
1 Scope 
This document specifies the core digitalization classification and methods selection, preparation 
work, digital data acquisition, data processing and interpretation, data collation, quality inspection, 
report preparation and data submission of core digitalization.  
This document is applicable to digitalization work of cores drilled for mineral exploration, oil & gas 
exploration, marine geology, hydrogeology-engineering-environmental geology, geoscience research 
and other types of geological works. 
2 Normative references 
There are no normative references in this document. 
3 Terms and definitions 
For the purposes of this document, the following terms and definitions apply. 
3.1  
core 
columnar rock sample drilled by drill bit 
[SOURCE: GB/T 9151—1988, 13.1.1] 
3.2 
core digitalization 
non-destructive (or micro-destructive) method used to acquire core surface and internal information 
which includes images, minerals, structures, textures, and physical properties, using detective and 
analysis technologies based on sound, optical, electric, magnetic, wave 
3.3 
core surface image digitalization 
method used to obtain core surface images with digital camera or large core image scanning system 
3.4 
core spectral scanning 
method used to obtain spectral data of core surface continuously with mechanical transmission 
device and spectrometer, process data in depth correction, invalid data elimination, data 
interpretation, and eventually produce spectral data and mineral information matched core depth 
3.5 
core digitalization for X-ray fluorescence element 
method used to continuously obtain element types and concentrations on core surface at a certain 
interval with X-ray fluorescence (XRF) analyzer 
3.6 
core multi-scale CT scanning 
method used to scan the core with X-ray source computerized tomography (CT) equipment, process 
and analyze the obtained projection data, obtain the core internal image based on the layer 
information, and establish the core internal 3-D structure

core digitalization for micro-region scanning electron microscope imaging 
method used to obtain high-resolution image of representative micro-regions of the core with 
electron microscope and technologies such as backscattered electron imaging, secondary electron 
microscopy, focused ion beam, and energy-dispersive spectroscopy, and extract information such as

3.8 
core digitalization for physical parameters 
method used to measure the physical properties of the core such as impedance, bulk reactance and 
magnetic strength by using physical property measuring equipment, and obtain the information of 
the resistivity, polarizability, magnetic susceptibility and density of the core 
4 Basic requirements 
4.1 Core classification and requirements 
4.1.1 According to different geological works, cores are classified into different types, including 
cores for mineral exploration, oil & gas exploration, marine geology, hydrogeology-engineering-
environmental geology, geoscience research and other types.  
4.1.2 Cores shall be preserved well in the field before digitalization. The cores with storage 
acceptance of the archive buildings shall be packed during transportation. The cores which tend to 
be weathered or fractured easily, shall be fixed to prevent any irreversible changes of core 
composition, structure, order, etc. 
4.1.3 Representative and typical cores which can reflect the features of the strata, lithology, 
paleontology, structure, minerals, and other features of geological mass in the study area shall be 
selected for digitalization. 
4.2 Core digitalization methods classification and requirements 
4.2.1 According to the information acquisition method, core digitalization methods is divided into 
surface image digitalization, spectral scanning, X-ray fluorescence element analysis, multi-scale CT 
scanning, micro-region scanning electron microscope imaging, physical parameters digitalization, 
etc.  
4.2.2 Digitalization methods shall be properly selected according to the purpose of core 
digitalization. 
4.2.3 Core digitalization workload and parameters including the interval of analysis spots, analysis 
duration, etc. shall meet the requirements of the follow-up geological survey, mineral exploration, 
and scientific research. 
4.2.4 Generally, cores are digitalized in units of drill hole (borehole). One drill hole (borehole) is a 
digitalization unit. 
4.3 Core digitalization workflow 
Core digitalization workflow consists of preparation work, digital data acquisition, data processing 
and interpretation, data collation, quality inspection, report preparation and data submission. 
4.4 Data sharing and services 
Core digitalization data shall be named and organized uniformly to establish dataset and provide in-
time services. 
5 Core digitalization method selection 
5.1 Cores for mineral exploration

5.1.1 Cores for mineral exploration refer to cores drilled from geological surveys, mineral 
exploration, and exploitation of metallic and non-metallic deposits. 
5.1.2 For the whole cores, surface image digitalization shall be conducted; for those cores from 
important drill holes, important layers and key sections, spectral scanning and XRF measurement 
shall also be conducted, and physical parameters such as resistivity, polarizability, magnetic 
susceptibility, and micro-region scanning electron microscope imaging should be conducted. 
5.1.3 Cores from important drill holes include but are not limited to 
a) cores from representative drill holes in main exploration lines which can reflect the geological 
characteristics of large-super large deposits of important minerals, 
b) cores from representative drill holes in main exploration lines of deposits with special 
significance which includes deposits with new genetic types, deposits with new minerals, and 
typical deposits, and 
c) cores from long drill holes (end depth not less than 2000 m) drilled during exploration in mining 
area. 
5.1.4  Cores from important layers and key sections include but are not limited to cores from 
mineralization belts, alteration bands, top and bottom plate of orebody, and other mineralization-
related sections. 
5.2 Cores for oil & gas exploration 
5.2.1 Cores for oil & gas exploration refer to cores drilled from geological survey, exploration, and 
exploitation for conventional oil & gas and non-conventional shale oil, shale gas, gas hydrate, and 
tight oil, coalbed methane, etc. 
5.2.2 For the whole cores, surface image digitalization, spectral scanning and XRF measurement 
shall be conducted; for those cores from important drill holes, important layers and key sections, 
multi-scale CT scanning shall be conducted and physical parameters such as electrical resistivity, 
polarizability, and micro-region scanning electron microscope imaging should be also conducted. For 
cores from oil & gas reservoir sections which tend to be weathered or fractured easily, multi-scale 
CT scanning and micro-region scanning electron microscope imaging shall be conducted before the 
core sections are damaged. 
5.2.3 Cores from important drill holes include but are not limited to cores from geological survey 
drill holes, regional exploration drill holes, parameter drill holes and marine oil & gas drill holes. 
Cores from important layers and key sections include but are not limited to cores from reservoir 
sections, capstone sections and hydrocarbon-generating sections. 
5.3 Cores for marine geology 
5.3.1 Cores for marine geology refer to sediment samples and cores drilled from regional marine 
geology survey, coastal geology survey, marine mineral resources survey, and marine geology survey 
in deep sea or ocean. 
5.3.2 When sediment samples get split for other analysis, surface image digitalization shall be 
conducted for split sediment samples, whilst the XRF and physical parameters measurement 
including electrical resistivity and magnetic susceptibility should be carried out at the same time. For 
the whole cores, surface image digitalization, spectral scanning, and XRF measurement shall be 
conducted, and physical parameters measurement such as electrical resistivity and magnetic 
susceptibility should be carried out; for important sediment samples and cores from important drill 
holes, important layers and key sections, multi-scale CT scanning may be conducted when necessary.

5.3.3 Important sediment samples and cores from important drill holes include but are not limited 
to  
a) sediment samples and cores from drill holes drilled in regional marine geology survey, 
b) representative sediment samples and cores from representative drill holes drilled in coastal 
geology survey, 
c) representative sediment samples and cores from representative drill holes drilled in marine 
mineral resources survey, and 
d) sediment samples and cores from drill holes drilled during marine geology survey in deep sea or 
ocean. 
5.3.4 Cores from important layers and key sections include but are not limited to cores from 
enrichment layers and layers involved in important geological environmental events. 
5.4 Cores for hydrogeology-engineering-environmental geology  
5.4.1 Cores for hydrogeology-engineering-environmental geology refer to cores drilled from 
hydrogeology-engineering-environmental geology, hazard geology, and urban geology works. 
5.4.2 Cores from important drill holes include but are not limited to 
a) cores from representative drill holes from hydrogeology exploration of large groundwater source 
field, 
b) cores from drill holes which represent major geological environmental evolution and 
environmental events and reflect the obvious comparison.  
c) cores from representative bedrock standard and stratification standard from ground subsidence 
exploration, 
d) cores from representative drill holes or long drill holes from important hot dry rock or 
geothermal exploration. 
5.4.3 Cores from important layers and key sections include but are not limited to cores from layers 
and sections in main tectonic belts, bearing layers, aquifers, geothermal resource occurrence layers, 
major environmental events occurrence layers, and cores with special significance to the hydrology-
engineering-environmental geology in working areas. 
5.4.4 For the whole cores, surface image digitalization shall be conducted; for cores from long drill 
holes and special drill holes from engineering geology exploration of major projects, spectral 
scanning and XRF measurement of the whole cores should be carried out at the same time and multi-
scale CT scanning of representative core sections may also be conducted when necessary. For cores 
from other important drill holes, important layers and key sections, spectral scanning and XRF 
measurement may be conducted when necessary. 
5.5 Cores for geoscience research 
5.5.1 Cores for geoscience research refer to cores drilled from scientific research, polar research, 
astrogeology research, and deep geology research. 
5.5.2 For the whole cores, surface image digitalization, spectral scanning, XRF and physical 
parameters measurement including electrical resistivity and magnetic susceptibility shall be 
conducted; for those cores from important layers and key sections, multi-scale CT scanning and 
micro-region scanning electron microscope imaging should be conducted at the same time. 
5.5.3 Cores from important layers and key sections include but are not limited to cores in layers 
and sections such as mark layers, contact boundaries, resource enrichment sections for scientific 
research.

5.6 Cores for other types 
5.6.1 Cores for other types refer to cores drilled from other geology work including regional 
geology survey and natural resources survey, etc. 
5.6.2 Surface image digitalization, spectral scanning, XRF measurement and other types of 
digitalization may be conducted when necessary, needed by digital preservation, scientific research 
or public services of cores.  
6  Preparation 
6.1 Preparation on equipment, work site and operator 
6.1.1 Large-scale core scanning equipment should be given preference to core digitalization. 
Portable devices may be used if the large-scale equipment is not available. All the equipment shall be 
verified or calibrated. 
6.1.2 The core digitalization work sites shall be clean, flat, spacious, and isolated from corrosive 
gases, without the interruption of dust and noise. Light, temperature and humidity shall meet the 
requirements of the equipment operation. 
6.1.3 The operators shall possess a certain amount of geological knowledge and be trained to 
operate the equipment. The suits shall meet the requirements of safety and cannot interfere with the 
equipment. 
6.2 Preparation on cores 
6.2.1 Place the core boxes in the order of depth from the top to the bottom successively. Make sure 
the axle of the core is basically in line. Rotate the core to make the marked surface downward. Place 
marked core labeling boards at sides of the box and place them back after digitalization. 
6.2.2 If the surface height of core sections is inconsistent, the core sections with lower height shall 
be uplifted to make all the core sections are at the same level to eliminate the disturbance caused by 
the inconsistency.  
6.2.3 Cores with dust, mud, and other materials shall be cleaned depending on their features as 
follows, 
a) generally, clean water should be used to wash the cores without damaging their original 
condition. Handle the cores carefully not to damage the labels and notes on the surface while 
cleaning. Dry the cores naturally after cleaning, 
b) for crushing cores, and cores containing water-soluble or clay minerals, soft brush and jet air 
shall be used to remove the dust on the surface and dry rags shall be used to clean the core surface, 
c) for oily cores, and cores from oil-based drilling fluid coring and sealed coring drill holes, non-
metal scrapers or cotton yarn shall be used to clean them, and 
d) split the cores which cannot be cleaned with bad contamination or damage and digitalize the 
new surface. 
6.2.4 The wooden bars or foam bars of the same length shall be placed at the missing core sections. 
The depth information of missing core sections shall be marked on the surface. Sediment cores for 
microbiological and organic analysis shall be refrigerated or frozen. Digitalization shall be completed 
as soon as possible to prevent obvious thawing caused by prolonged exposure.

7 Digital data acquisition  
7.1 Before the data acquisition, the drill hole (borehole) basic information table shall be filled in, 
as specified in Annex A.  
7.2 In the process of the data acquisition, data shall be monitored in real time. The abnormal issues 
shall be solved in time before resuming the digitalization. For details, see other parts of DD 2022-1.  
7.3 Cores shall be stored properly after placing them and their labelling boards back to the original 
place after digitalization. 
7.4 After data acquisition, data processing shall be conducted, which includes but is not limited to 
image correction, image clipping and stitching, invalid data elimination. Data interpretation may be 
carried out if necessary. For details, see other parts of DD 2022-1. 
7.5 After data acquisition, the data shall be transformed into uniform format as required. For 
details, see other parts of DD 2022-1. 
8 Data collation 
8.1 Establish three-level folders for core digital data collation. Data files shall be saved in 
corresponding folders. 
8.2 Level 1 folders are named as "project code _ English abbreviation of data type". Level 2 folders 
are named as "drill hole (borehole) code or placeholder _English abbreviation of data type". 
8.3 Level 3 folders consist of folders for original data, processed data and interpreted data based 
on process levels of data. Folders for original data are named as “drill hole (borehole) code or 
placeholder_L0_ English abbreviation of data type”. Folders for processed data are named as “drill 
hole (borehole) code_L1_ English abbreviation of data type”. Folders for interpreted data are named 
as “drill hole (borehole) code or placeholder _L2_ English abbreviation of data type”.  
8.4 The English abbreviation of core surface images is “IMG”, of spectral scanning data is “SPE”, of 
core X-ray fluorescence data is “XRF”, of core multi-scale CT structure data is “MCT”, of micro-region 
scanning electron microscope images is “SEM”, and of core physical parameter data is “PHY”.  
8.5 All the data and records generated in core digitalization shall be backed up in at least 3 
different kinds of storage devices.  
9 Quality inspection 
9.1 All the data generated in core digitalization shall be inspected. The self-inspection and mutual-
inspection rate shall be up to 100%, and the sampling inspection rate shall not be less than 10%. The 
inspection rate may be raised according to the requirements of different digitalization methods. 
9.2 Completeness, normalization and accuracy of the data are checked as follows, 
a) completeness refers to whether all mandatory data is complete, whether each data is intact, and 
whether there’s data missing or damage, 
b) normalization refers to whether the format, naming and organization of data is normative, and 
c) accuracy refers to whether the data could reflect the real condition of the cores. 
9.3 Record all quality issues exactly. Analyze and eliminate the causes of these issues in time. 
Digitalize cores again if necessary.

10 Report preparation and data submission 
10.1 Core digitalization report shall be prepared after digitalization. Relevant information, see 
Annex B. The outline may be modified according to the requirements of different digitalization 
methods. 
10.2 Core digital data along with record tables and core digitalization reports shall be submitted, 
listed as follows, 
a) core digitalization technical report (in paper and electronic), 
b) dataset and metadata including the original data, processed data and interpreted data, and 
c) all kinds of record tables (in paper and electronic) containing core digitalization process records 
and quality inspection records.
