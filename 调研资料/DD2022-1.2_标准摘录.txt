Introduction 
Cores are the fundamental material for understanding the underground geological information. The 
surface and internal information of cores such as images, minerals, elements, structures, textures, 
physical properties, and other information can be obtained non-destructively (or micro-
destructively), continuously, fast, and in high-resolution by core digitalization. Core digitalization can 
achieve the digital data preservation and efficient utilization of geological cores, which can provide 
technical services and data supports for geological survey, mineral resources exploration, ecological 
environment assessment, geological science research, and disaster prevention and mitigation, etc. 
DD 2022-1 is a series of basic and general standards, which can be used as the guidance on core 
digitalization for the core storage organizations and project undertakers. Considering the different 
working principles, methods, and requirements of various types of core digitization technologies, DD 
2022-1 is planned to consist of the following seven parts: 
——Part 1: General, which establishes the general principles and basic requirements for core 
digitalization; 
——Part 2: Surface image digitalization, which establishes operational, traceable and verifiable 
procedures and requirements for core surface image acquisition; 
——Part 3: Spectral scanning, which establishes operational, traceable and verifiable procedures and 
requirements for collecting core spectral reflectance and mineral information; 
——Part 4: X-ray fluorescence element, which establishes operational, traceable and verifiable 
procedures and requirements for collecting core elemental information; 
——Part 5: Multi-scale CT scanning, which establishes operational, traceable and verifiable 
procedures and requirements for collecting core surface and internal structural information; 
——Part 6: Micro-region scanning electron microscope imaging, which establishes operational, 
traceable and verifiable procedures and requirements for collecting core micro-regional surface and 
internal information about mineral, element and structure; 
——Part 7: Physical parameters, which establishes operational, traceable and verifiable procedures 
and requirements for collecting core physical information about resistivity, magnetic susceptibility, 
etc. 
The core surface images reflect the basic geological information of the cores. The surface color, 
texture, mineral composition, oil-bearing characteristics, etc. of partial cores have undergone 
significant changes, due to weathering, leaching and other reasons during long-term storage, which 
make it difficult to present their original appearances. The first-hand core surface image information 
can be obtained by core digitization in a timely and standardized manner, to establish a database. 
Based on the online services, the remote query and loan of the cores can be realized, which in turn 
promotes the sharing and utilization of core digital resources.

1 Scope 
This document specifies the basic requirements and operating methods for core preparation, image 
acquisition, image processing, data collation, quality inspection, report preparation and data 
submission of core surface image digitalization. 
This document is applicable to core surface image digitalization, including core surface image 
photography and core true color image scanning for various types of cores generated from mineral 
geology, petroleum geology, hydrogeology, engineering geology and environmental geology work. 
2 Normative references 
The following normative referenced documents are indispensable for the application of this 
document. For dated references, only the edition cited applies. For undated references, the latest 
edition of the referenced document (including any amendments) applies. 
DD 2022—1.1 Code of practice for core digitalization - Part 1: General 
3 Terms and definitions 
For the purposes of this document, the terms and definitions given in DD 2022-1.1 and the following 
apply. 
3.1 
core image photography 
process of capturing the image information on the upper surface of the cores in a single box using a 
digital camera or a mobile phone 
3.2 
core true color image scanning 
process of continuously collecting the core surface image information using a desktop core image 
scanner  
3.3 
core transitional true color image scanning 
process of continuously collecting the image information on the upper surface of the cores in a single 
box using a desktop core image scanner 
3.4 
core rolling true color image scanning 
process of continuously collecting the circumferential surface image information of a single columnar 
core using a desktop core image scanner 
3.5 
white balance 
process of making the image radiation with relative spectral energy distribution consistent with the 
subject's radiation when the subject's light source is compensated to be visually achromatic by 
adjusting the electronic image color channel gain or through image processing.

4 Basic requirements 
4.1 Core surface image digitalization work process mainly include preparation, core image 
acquisition, image processing, data collation and organization, quality inspection, report preparation 
and data submission . 
4.2 For the cores drilled at the field drilling site, core surface image photography shall be carried out 
in time after cleaning and before sampling. 
4.3 For the cores that have been screened and stored in the geological archives, core true color image 
scanning shall be carried out in time on the basis of the core surface image photography at the field 
drilling site. 
4.4 For the columnar samples of seabed sediments encapsulated by casings, core surface image 
photography or core true color image scanning shall be carried out after splitting and before 
sampling when splitting casings for testing and analyzing samples. 
4.5 The images shall be looked as closely to those observed with the naked eyes as possible, while 
the brightness and color shall not be distorted. 
4.6 The images shall be clear, with which the main rock-forming minerals, tectonic structures, 
mineralization alteration and other geological features can be clearly observed. The image resolution 
of a digital camera or mobile phone shall not be less than 100 PPI, the image resolution of a core 
image scanner shall not be less than 300 DPI, and the clarity shall not be less than 24-bit true color. 
5 Preparation 
5.1 Equipment preparation 
5.1.1 Before carrying out core surface image photography or core true color image scanning, the 
equipment shall be checked. After assuring that the equipment functions well, core surface image 
photography or core true color image scanning can be carried out. 
5.1.2 The cut-and-try core surface image photography or core true color image scanning shall be 
carried out with the normal-performance equipment, and the samples shall be observed and 
evaluated to confirm whether the equipment meet the requirements. If the color of the sample is 
distorted, a standard whiteboard shall be photographed or scanned to obtain the standard 
whiteboard images, with which the white balance test and chromaticity calibration of the equipment 
could be conducted applied with the white balance setting software that comes with the equipment. 
5.2 Site preparation 
5.2.1 Core surface image photography shall be carried out at flat places with sufficient light and 
without direct sunlight. If supplementary light sources are required, daylight lamps shall be used. 
5.2.2 The core true color image scanning site temperature shall be controlled between 10℃ and 
30℃ , and the relative humidity shall be controlled between 30% and 70%. The equipment shall not 
be placed under the environment exposed to vibration, humidity and direct sunlight. When scanning 
with a white light source, the site shall be with sufficient and soft light, and the illuminance on the 
core surface shall not be lower than 750 lx, and when scanning with a fluorescent light source, a dark 
room environment shall be created by using a hood to ensure that the scanning is not disturbed by 
other light sources.

5.3 Technicians preparation 
5.3.1 Technicians shall be trained on equipment operation, and quality inspection, etc. 
5.3.2 Technicians shall have operated the equipment accompanied and guided by the skillful 
operators who have been operating the equipment for no less than 40 h. 
5.4 Cores preparation 
Sort and clean the cores according to DZ/T 0441.1-2023 to ensure that the cores are tightly butted 
in the correct order, the core surface is clean, and the central axis is basically on a straight line before 
carrying out core surface image photography or core true color image scanning. 
6  Core image acquisition 
6.1 Core surface image photography 
6.1.1 Arrange the core in ascending order of the drilling depth and ensure that the core box is 
placed horizontally with the starting depth at the upper left corner and the ending depth at the lower 
right corner. 
6.1.2 Lay the core tags and run number labels horizontally on the lower part of each run for easy 
information access at a later stage. 
6.1.3 Place the scale at the lower right corner of the core box and the standard color card should 
be placed at the same time. 
6.1.4 Set the mode, adjust the angle, and focus the lens according to the equipment instructions. 
6.1.5 Adjust the field of view to ensure that the core image accounts for more than 90% of the entire 
field of view. 
6.1.6 A bench should be used to fix the equipment just above the core to ensure that the optical 
axis of the camera is perpendicular to the core surface; the remote control button should be used to 
start the shooting procedure, which can remove jitter impacts on the image acquisition quality. 
6.1.7 Such operations shall be done as enlarging the image to check whether the image of the cores 
and the round-trip sign could meet the requirements of 4.4 and 4.5, after completing the core images 
acquisition in a whole box, and if not, or the image of the round-trip sign is not clear, photography 
shall be carried out again. 
6.1.8 The core surface image photography record (as specified in Annex A) shall be filled in 
synchronously when carrying out core surface image photography. 
6.2 Core true color image scanning  
6.2.1 Instrument warm-up 
The core true color image scanner shall be warmed up before put into use, and start scanning until 
the performance and light source of the instrument are stable. 
6.2.2 Light source and scanning method selection 
Select the light source (including white light and fluorescence) and the scanning method (including 
translational image scanning and rolling image scanning) reasonably according to the core types and 
research purposes. White light source is suitable for all cores, and fluorescent light source is suitable 
for cores containing organic materials; translational image scanning is suitable for all cores, and 
rolling image scanning is suitable for columnar cores from important core sections. 
6.2.3 Scanner parameter setting 
6.2.3.1 Measure the size of the core box, and enter the data such as the length, width and channel

number of the core box into the scanning software of the scanner. The size shall not exceed the 
maximum range. 
6.2.3.2 Set the contrast, brightness and resolution. It is recommended the contrast and brightness be 
the default values, and the resolution shall meet the requirement of Clause 4.6 of this document. The 
resolution for cores from the important drill holes, layers and key sections could be appropriately 
enhanced to 400 DPI to 800 DPI. The selection of the cores from the important drill holes, layers and 
key sections shall meet the requirements of DZ/T 0441.1-2023, Clause 5. 
6.2.4 Light source adjustment 
Adjust the aperture of the lens, the height of the lamp holder or the brightness of the light according 
to the light conditions and the color of the cores. Check the acquired testing core images to make 
them looked as closely as possible to those images observed with naked eyes. The requirements are 
as follows: 
a) for dark-color cores with low reflectance, such as carbonaceous mudstone, mafic or ultramafic 
igneous, etc., the aperture shall be appropriately enlarged or the height of the lamp holder shall 
be lowered or the brightness of the light shall be enhanced to improve the brightness of the 
acquired images to prevent minerals from being unidentifiable due to dark images, 
b) for light-color cores with high reflectance, such as sandstone, limestone, kaolin, mica schist, etc., 
the aperture shall be appropriately reduced or the height of the lamp holder shall be lifted or the 
brightness of the light shall be reduced to prevent the acquired images from being distorted due 
to overexposure, and 
c) for cores composed of light-color and dark-color minerals, the size of the aperture, the height of 
the lamp holder or the brightness of the light shall be adjusted, on the basis that the lightest-color 
minerals are not overexposed. Meanwhile, the content of light-color minerals shall be considered 
at the same time. However, if the light-color minerals only incidentally occurred, the content 
might not be considered. 
6.2.5 Core translational true color image scanning  
Carry out core translational true color image scanning according to the following workflows: 
a) adjust the lamp holder of the scanner, to make it perpendicular to the travel direction of the lens, 
b) place the core box on the workbench, parallel to the travel direction of the lens, 
c) adjust the scanning lens by using the scanning lift control button and the fixed focal ruler. The 
scanning lens shall focus on the core surface, and if the core surface is uneven, the average height 
shall prevail, 
d) start the scanning work, and shall check whether the images could meet the requirements of 4.4 
and 4.5, and if not, reset the scanner or re-scan the cores, and 
e) shall fill in the core true color image scanning record table (as specified in Annex B) 
synchronously when carrying out core translational true color image scanning. 
6.2.6 Core rolling true color image scanning 
Carry out core rolling true color image scanning according to the following workflows: 
a) adjust the lamp holder of the scanner to make it perpendicular to the travel direction of the lens, 
b) place the core flat on the two rubber rollers of the scanner and adjust the width of the rubber 
rollers to make sure that the core rotates uniformly around the central axis, 
c) adjust the scanning lens by using the scanning lift control button and the fixed focal ruler. The

d) enter the scanning length and other information and then start rolling scanning. For the single 
long core, multiple scanning shall be done section by section to ensure the integrity of the 
acquired images, and each scanning shall have no less than 5% repetition of the previous 
scanning, 
e) shall check whether the images could meet the requirements of 4.4 and 4.5 during the scanning

7 Core image processing 
7.1 Photographed core surface image processing 
7.1.1 The acquired photographed core surface images shall be rectified and adjusted on the 
brightness, contrast and saturation, after completing the shooting of the cores from one drill hole 
(borehole), to make the images looked as closely to those observed with the naked eyes as possible. 
7.1.2 Crop the acquired single-box core images along the outer edge of the core box to remove 
redundant parts. 
7.1.3 The processed image files shall be named as "drill hole (borehole) no._core box no.".  
EXAMPLE: The image files of the first box of the drill hole ZK001 shall be named as “ZK001_001”. 
7.1.4 The core surface image photography record (as specified in Annex A) shall be filled in 
synchronously when carrying out photographed core surface images processing. 
7.2 Core true color scanning image processing 
7.2.1 The acquired core scanning images shall be rectified and adjusted including the brightness, 
contrast and saturation, after completing the scanning of the cores from one drill hole (borehole), to 
make the images looked as closely to those observed with the naked eyes as possible. 
7.2.2 The single-box core images can be obtained by translational scanning, which shall be cropped 
according to the roundtrip (core barrel). The single core image can be obtained by rolling image 
scanning. If a single core is long, multiple scanning shall be done section by section and the scanned 
multi-section original images shall be stitched with the images mosaic software. Manual fine-tuning 
shall be carried out with reference to the surface texture and mineral distribution of the cores to 
ensure that there is no obvious splicing trace in the single core image after mosaic. 
7.2.3 The processed image file acquired by translational image scanning of a single core shall be 
named as “HC + roundtrip no.”. When one roundtrip of the cores occupies two or more compartments 
in the core boxes, the image file after mosaic consists of multiple images, which needs to be 
distinguished by adding suffix names.   
EXAMPLE: The first core image file of the fifth roundtrip shall be named as “HC5_1”. 
7.2.4 The single core image file obtained by rolling image scanning shall be named as “core box 
no._compartment no._core serial no.”. 
EXAMPLE: the image file of the third core in the second compartment in the fourth core box shall be named as 
“4_3_2”.

cropping or mosaic, according to the Formula (1).

8 Data collation 
8.1 Core image data 
8.1.1 Core image data files shall be organized in three-level folders, including the image data of all 
drill holes of the project, the image data of a single drill hole, and the original image data of a single 
drill hole (L0), or the processed image data of a single drill hole (L1) after image cropping and mosaic. 
8.1.2 Level 1 folder is used to store the core image data of all drill holes of the project. The folder 
shall be named as "project code _IMG". 
EXAMPLE: the core image data of DD2020009 project shall be named as "DD2020009_IMG". 
8.1.3 Level 2 folder is used to store all the photographed or scanning image data of the drill hole. 
The folder shall be named as "drill hole number_IMG". 
EXAMPLE: the image data of drill hole ZK01 shall be named as "ZK01_IMG". 
8.1.4 Level 3 folder consists of the original core image data folder of the drill hole (L0) and the 
processed core image data folder of the drill hole (L1), storing the original core image data obtained 
by the scanner and the processed image data after image cropping and mosaic respectively. The two 
folders shall be named as "drill hole number_L0_IMG" and "drill hole number_L1_IMG" respectively. 
EXAMPLE: ZK01 original core image data shall be named as "ZK01_L0_IMG". 
8.1.5 The core image data shall be in JPG or TIFF format stored in the corresponding folder. 
8.2 Core digitalization images record table 
8.2.1 All the core photographing or scanning images record tables shall be examined to check 
whether the recording depth, the roundtrip information, and the core length, etc. are inaccurate, 
missing or redundant. 
8.2.2 Arrange the paper core photographing or scanning images record tables of each drill hole 
(borehole) according to the sequence of recording depth from the upper part to the lower part, and 
bind together into book form.  
8.2.3 Enter the paper core photographing or scanning images record tables into the computer to 
generate the electronic form.

9 Quality inspection 
9.1 General requirements 
9.1.1 The data quality shall be inspected, and the quality inspection record table (as specified in 
Annex C) shall be filled in after finishing the core image acquisition and processing. 
9.1.2 Quality inspection consists of self-inspection, mutual inspection and sampling inspection. 100% 
self-inspection and mutual inspection shall be conducted throughout the core image photography 
and core true color image scanning, and at least 20% sampling inspection shall be conducted.  
9.1.3 Quality inspection shall be conducted from the aspects of completeness, normalization, and 
accuracy of the data and documents.  
9.1.4 The quality problems identified during the inspection such as image blurriness shall be 
recorded in a timely manner and the causes shall be investigated. For problems that cannot be 
corrected (see 9.4.1, 9.4.2 and 9.4.3 for details), the scanning (photography) work shall be re-
conducted. 
9.2 Completeness inspection 
9.2.1 Inspect whether all the record tables of core image photography and core true color image 
scanning are filled in completely and accurately. 
9.2.2 Inspect whether all the original core images and the processed core images by cropping or 
mosaic are complete. 
9.2.3 Inspect whether the image data is complete, and whether there are missing parts needed to 
be photographed or scanned. 
9.3 Normalization inspection 
Inspect whether various levels of folders meet the requirements, and whether the names of various 
files are normative. 
9.4 Accuracy inspection 
9.4.1 Inspect whether the images are  looked as closely to those observed with the naked eyes, and 
if there is uneven brightness or color distortion, the scanning (photography) work shall be re-
conducted. 
9.4.2 Inspect whether the images are clear and whether the major geological features such as rock-
forming minerals, textures and structures, and mineralization and alteration can be clearly observed, 
and if the images are blurry, the scanning (photography) work shall be re-conducted. 
9.4.3 Inspect whether the round-trip labels are clear after capturing the images, and if the round-
trip labels  are blurry, photography work shall be re-conducted. 
9.4.4 Inspect whether the images correspond one-to-one with the actual cores, and that the 
information in the record tables is consistent with the core images and other relevant information.
